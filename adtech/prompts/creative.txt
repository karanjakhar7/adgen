Craft rules:
- ONE idea per ad. Lead with the message_angle above and commit to it. Do NOT cram in other value props — a second idea weakens the first.
- Headline's job: hook the persona's core motivation on that single angle, in their own framing. Make them feel it's about them. Max ~12 words.
- Body's job: earn the click. One concrete proof or vivid specific (a number, a sensory detail, a named moment, the persona's internal monologue) — not adjectives. Then hand off to the CTA. 1–2 sentences.
- CTA: a short, specific next step matched to the advertiser's purchase_objective (e.g. trial → "Start your free trial"; subscription → "Subscribe & save"; purchase → "Shop the senior formulas"). Never generic "Shop now and save!".
- Match the PLACEMENT: the same offer is pitched differently on impulse, late-night, mobile inventory than on considered, affluent, high-AOV inventory. Mirror that environment's tempo and the audience's mindset; honor any tonal cue in the publisher's notes (e.g. "playful voice converts best", "skeptical of unsubstantiated claims").
- Hold the advertiser's brand_voice across the whole variant — the persona shapes WHAT resonates, brand_voice governs HOW it sounds. They must coexist.

Hard constraints:
- USE the persona's messaging_preferences — they are the voice and claim style this person responds to.
- RESPECT disinterested_in as hard constraints. If the persona rejects influencer positioning or loud aesthetics, your copy must contain none of it.
- Ground every claim in the advertiser's value_props and proof_points. Do not invent product facts, statistics, endorsements, or guarantees the brief does not support.
- Respect the advertiser's negative_constraints.
- If sensitive_category_flags contains "health_claim", use supportive, non-medical phrasing ("supports joint comfort", "made for senior dogs") — never curative or guaranteed-outcome claims ("cures arthritis", "eliminates pain").
